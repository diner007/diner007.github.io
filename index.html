Formal Verification and Assurance of AI Systems and Hardware

I am an Associate Professor of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at the University of Missouri and Director of the Dependable Cyber-Physical Systems (DCPS) Laboratory. My research focuses on developing formal methods, verification techniques, and runtime assurance frameworks to improve the safety, security, and reliability of AI-enabled systems and hardware. My work spans trustworthy AI, Physical AI, dependable and resource-efficient AI hardware, and cyber-physical systems, with applications in autonomous systems and critical infrastructure.

About

From specifications
to evidence.

How can we establish rigorous assurances about the safety, security, and reliability of AI-enabled systems? Our research develops formal methods and verification techniques to specify, analyze, and verify critical system properties. We combine mathematical reasoning, temporal logic, and runtime verification to advance the design of trustworthy and dependable computing systems.

Our work spans trustworthy AI, Physical AI, cyber-physical systems, hardware security, and dependable computing, with a focus on safety-critical applications, autonomous systems, edge intelligence, and critical infrastructure. We develop specification-driven methods and tools that bridge formal guarantees with practical system design and deployment.

I am a senior member of IEEE and ACM.

Research capabilities

COMPASS: Specification-guided LLM compression

COMPASS uses signal temporal logic to guide variable-bit compression, verify agent-generated hardware designs, and generate on-chip monitors. In the reported evaluation, it reduced stored weight bits by a factor of 2.73 versus FP16, with a 3.1% increase in perplexity.

IEEE DATE 2027 · Accepted, forthcoming

Trustworthy AI at the edge

Verify that compressed foundation models preserve specified behavior on resource-constrained devices. VeriFAI integrates these checks into model compression so designers can assess trade-offs between assurance and resource use.

VeriFAI project

Resilient, energy-efficient AI hardware

Evaluate accelerator reliability and design hardware for lower power use and reuse. In its reported tests on billion-parameter models, RIFT assessed faults 2.2× faster than evolutionary methods.

RIFT paper and benchmarks

Safety, security & privacy in immersive systems

Evaluate cyber resilience and privacy risks in extended reality (XR). We also use explainable AI to predict cybersickness and test adaptive mitigation on immersive systems.

RelaxVR code and evaluation data

Physical AI & autonomous systems

Study how autonomous systems can act safely in physical environments. We use temporal logic to express timing and coordination requirements and check whether robotic motion plans satisfy them.

Motion planning paper

Formal verification & runtime assurance

Specify timing and safety requirements for autonomous cyber-physical systems. We use temporal logic to check motion plans and monitor whether a system meets time-bounded requirements during operation.

Runtime monitoring paper
